Degree of Freedom

A rigid body moves in six independent ways: three translations along axes and three rotations about them. Every flight is a mix of these six.

SurgeSwayHeaveRollPitchYaw

Linear Motions

  • Surge: forward and back along the x axis
  • Sway: side to side along the y axis
  • Heave: up and down along the z axis

Forces Acting on Aircraft

  • Lift up, Weight down
  • Thrust forward, Drag back

Steady level flight needs lift = weight and thrust = drag. Set thrust to 50 for balance, then push it up or down.

Lift and Thrust in Rotor-based Aircraft

Rotors push air down. Thrust acts perpendicular to the rotor disk. Hover when thrust = weight. Tilting splits thrust into lift (T cos θ) and forward thrust (T sin θ).

Lift and Thrust in Fixed-wing Aircraft

The wing deflects air to make lift, which needs forward speed. The propeller or jet gives thrust forward, only to beat drag. Raise the angle of attack: lift grows until the wing stalls near 15°.

Rotational Motions

  • Roll: about the x axis, wing tips up and down
  • Pitch: about the y axis, nose up and down
  • Yaw: about the z axis, nose left and right

Torques Acting on Aircraft

Torque = force × distance from the center of gravity. Control surfaces make force far from it: ailerons roll, elevator pitches, rudder yaws. Deflect a surface and the aircraft rotates until the restoring moment balances the torque.

Control Systems in Aircraft

Sensors measure the attitude, a controller compares it with the target, and actuators move the surfaces. Turn the autopilot off and hit a gust to see the difference.
